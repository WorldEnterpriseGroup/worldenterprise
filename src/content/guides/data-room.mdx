A data room is a shared, organized folder of the records someone needs to check a company's claims. Investors ask for one during due diligence, which is the structured check made before money moves. At seed stage it does not need special software. A shared drive with clear names and an index does the job.

Building the folder early is useful even if nobody asks for it. The act of filling it shows you what is missing while you still have time to fix it, and it replaces a scramble with a link.

## Principles

- **Organized.** A stranger should find any document in under a minute using the index alone.
- **Current.** An out-of-date document is worse than a missing one, because it makes everything else look doubtful. Put a date on every file.
- **True.** Include only records that describe the company as it is. A plan is labelled as a plan.
- **Permissioned.** Decide who can see which folder before you share. Share the minimum, in stages.
- **Yours.** Keep the master copy. Give viewers read-only access to a copy.

## Keep it honest

If a record is missing, leave a short note in the index that says what is missing and when you expect it. A visible gap with a date is easier to trust than a quiet one. Never fill a folder with documents you hope will pass. Anyone reading it is looking for the places where the story and the paperwork disagree.

Do not put secrets in the folder: passwords, private keys and personal data about customers or staff do not belong in a data room. If a document contains personal data, ask your counsel what must be removed before it is shared.

## Before you share it

1. Read the index as if you were a stranger. Can you find the cap table and the assignments of work in one step?
2. Check the dates. Replace or archive anything out of date.
3. Open each folder in a private window to confirm the permissions are what you intended.
4. Ask your own counsel to review folders 1, 5, 6 and 8 before they go anywhere.
5. Keep a log of who was given access and when.
