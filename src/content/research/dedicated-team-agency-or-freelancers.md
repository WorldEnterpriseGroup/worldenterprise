## Disclosure and label

This brief is **analysis**. It reasons from criteria and trade-offs; it does not report a study, and it contains no statistics. World Enterprise Group sells dedicated team seats, which is one of the three models compared. The criteria are set out so that you can test any model, including ours, and reach your own answer.

## The three models

These definitions are working definitions for this brief, not terms from a source.

- **Freelancers.** Individual contractors engaged directly by you. You find, brief, manage and replace each person.
- **Agency.** A firm engaged to deliver a scoped outcome. The agency staffs, schedules and manages its own people; you manage the scope and acceptance.
- **Dedicated team.** A group of people assigned to you for a continuing period, steered and managed by the provider, scalable up or down as your needs change.

## Legal basics that apply before any comparison

Three public documents set facts that shape the comparison. This is not legal advice.

**Who counts as a contractor.** The IRS states that evidence of the relationship falls into three categories: behavioral control (whether the company has "the right to control what the worker does and how the worker does his or her job"), financial control, and the type of relationship, including written contracts and whether the relationship will continue. It adds that "the keys are to look at the entire relationship and consider the extent of the right to direct and control the worker" [(IRS)](#source-irs-classification). The Department of Labor maintains separate guidance under the Fair Labor Standards Act; its page refers to a final rule published January 10, 2024, effective March 11, 2024, and to 29 CFR Part 795, and the current state of that guidance should be checked before relying on it [(DOL)](#source-dol-misclassification).

**Who owns the work.** US copyright law defines a work made for hire as a work prepared by an employee within the scope of employment, or a specially ordered or commissioned work in listed categories "if the parties expressly agree in a written instrument signed by them that the work shall be considered a work made for hire." A transfer of copyright ownership must be "in writing and signed by the owner of the copyright, or the owner's duly authorized agent" [(Copyright Office)](#source-usc17). Whatever the model, ownership of what is made should be settled in a signed writing.

**What security expectation travels with the work.** The SSDF offers "a common vocabulary for secure software development" [(SSDF)](#source-ssdf) and the ASVS describes security requirements "for designing, developing, and testing modern web applications and services" [(ASVS)](#source-asvs-site). Analysis: both can be attached to any staffing model as acceptance criteria, so security should not decide the model by itself.

## Ten criteria

1. **Control of method.** How much you want to direct how the work is done. High control resembles the behavioral-control category above and raises classification questions for individual contractors.
2. **Continuity.** Whether the same people stay long enough to hold context.
3. **Accountability.** Who answers for quality and delivery, and whether that is one party or several.
4. **Management load.** How much of your time goes to briefing, coordinating and replacing people.
5. **Scaling.** How quickly capacity can be raised or lowered.
6. **Breadth of skills.** Whether you need design, engineering, testing and operations together.
7. **Ownership of work.** How simply a signed writing can settle who owns what is made.
8. **Security acceptance.** How easily a common set of acceptance criteria can be applied and checked.
9. **Cost shape.** Whether spend is per scoped outcome, per person or per period. No figures are given here because none are sourced.
10. **Exit.** How easily you can stop and take the work with you.

## Trade-offs, model by model

**Freelancers** fit narrow, well-specified tasks where you already know what good looks like. They are strong on control of method and exit when work is small. They are weaker on continuity, breadth, and accountability, because each person answers for their own piece and you are the coordinator. Direct control of how an individual works is also the point where the classification tests above matter most.

**An agency** fits a defined outcome with a clear acceptance test. It is strong on breadth and on single-party accountability for the scope. It is weaker where requirements change often, because changes pass through scope and commercial terms, and weaker on continuity if staff rotate between clients.

**A dedicated team** fits continuing product work where the direction changes as you learn. It is strong on continuity, scaling by notice, breadth and a single party that manages the people. It is weaker on up-front cost clarity, because the commitment is a continuing one, and it needs you to be able to set direction rather than to hand over a finished specification. The provider, not you, manages and steers the team, so you give up direct control of method in exchange for lower management load.

## Reading the matrix

No model wins on every criterion. The useful move is to weight the criteria for your situation first, then read the matrix. A company with a fixed, finished specification weights acceptance and cost shape; a company still discovering its product weights continuity and scaling.

## Methodology

The three models are defined above. The ten criteria were derived by listing the questions a founder must answer when comparing models and removing duplicates. Ratings in the appendix are judgements by World Enterprise Editorial and are labelled as such. External documents were retrieved on 6 October 2026 and are cited only for the specific facts quoted.

## Limitations

- There is no survey, cost or performance data behind the ratings; none was sourced and none is invented.
- The ratings assume competent providers within each model. Poor providers fail in every model.
- The DOL guidance may have changed since the page was read; verify before relying on it.
- The author's firm sells one of the compared models.
- This is not legal advice. Worker classification and ownership depend on facts and jurisdiction.

## Appendix: decision matrix and question list

Ratings are judgement: Stronger, Mixed or Weaker for the model on that criterion, assuming competent providers.

| Criterion | Freelancers | Agency | Dedicated team |
|---|---|---|---|
| Control of method | Stronger | Weaker | Mixed |
| Continuity | Weaker | Mixed | Stronger |
| Accountability | Weaker | Stronger | Stronger |
| Management load on you | Weaker | Mixed | Stronger |
| Scaling | Mixed | Mixed | Stronger |
| Breadth of skills | Weaker | Stronger | Stronger |
| Ownership of work (signed writing needed in all) | Mixed | Stronger | Stronger |
| Security acceptance criteria | Mixed | Stronger | Stronger |
| Cost clarity up front | Mixed | Stronger | Weaker |
| Exit | Stronger | Mixed | Mixed |

Questions to answer before choosing:

1. Is the work a finished specification or an evolving product?
2. Who will set direction day to day, and do they have the time?
3. Which skills must work together, and how many people is that?
4. Where will ownership of the work be written down, and who signs?
5. Which security acceptance criteria will you attach, and who checks them?
6. What does stopping look like, and what do you take with you?
