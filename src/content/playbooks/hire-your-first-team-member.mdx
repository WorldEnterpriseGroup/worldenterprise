  - title: Write the outcome the role owns
    body: Describe what will be true when this person does the job well, in plain terms and without a list of tools. If you cannot name the outcome, you are not ready to write the job description.
  - title: Settle the working relationship with your advisers
    body: Decide with your counsel and accountant whether the person will be an employee, a contractor or something else. Bring the facts about how the work will be directed and paid, because the answer depends on them.
  - title: Write a job description a candidate can answer
    body: State the outcome, the first tasks, who the person works with and how you will judge the result. Remove requirements that are habits of the last company you worked for.
  - title: Interview every candidate the same way
    body: Use the same questions and the same scoring notes for everyone, and add a small piece of real work. Decide before the interviews what a strong answer sounds like.

    body: Write what the person is being offered, what the person is expected to do and what happens if either side ends the arrangement. Have your counsel review the terms before you send them.

    body: Arrange access to tools, one page that explains how the team works, a first piece of work with a clear result, and a person to ask. Ask your counsel and accountant what paperwork the first day requires.

This playbook does not give employment, tax or immigration advice. It helps you prepare the role and the facts, so your own counsel and accountant can tell you how to engage the person.

## What good looks like

A good first hire starts with a clear outcome, a fair process and a written arrangement. The new person can say in the first week what they are to deliver and how you will judge it.

- The outcome the role owns is written down, and the founders agree on it.
- Your counsel and accountant have been told the facts about how the work will be directed and paid, and you have their answer in writing.
- Every candidate answered the same questions, and the scoring notes were written before the decision.
- The offer is in writing, and counsel reviewed it before it was sent.
- The first week has a plan with one piece of real work and a named person to ask.

<Callout title="Illustrative example, not a real company">

A founder of a small inventory tool for bakeries has done all the support and the customer setup herself. She writes one page that says the first hire will own customer setup, so that every new bakery reaches its first working report. She tells her accountant and her lawyer how the work will be directed, whether the person will set their own hours and who will supply the equipment. She asks both how to engage the person. She interviews three candidates with the same four questions and a one-hour task based on a real setup. The offer goes to her lawyer before it goes to the candidate.

</Callout>

## Facts to know before you ask

The IRS says that facts that provide evidence of the degree of control and independence fall into three categories, which it labels behavioral, financial and type of relationship. Bring the facts in the table above to your counsel and accountant, who can weigh them for your case.

For an employee, USCIS describes a step to complete and sign Section 2 of Form I-9, Employment Eligibility Verification, within 3 business days of the date of hire of the employee. Ask your counsel or accountant whether this applies to your first hire and who will complete it.

## Evidence to check before you move on

Move to <MaybeLink path="/playbooks/price-your-first-saas-product/">Price your first SaaS product</MaybeLink> when you can show each of these.

- The outcome the role owns is written down and agreed by the founders.
- Your counsel and accountant have given you the advice on how to engage the person.
- The interview scorecard exists and was used for every candidate.
- The offer or agreement is written and reviewed by your counsel.
- The first-week plan exists and names the first piece of work.
