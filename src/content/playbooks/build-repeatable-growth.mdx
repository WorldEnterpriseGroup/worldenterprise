    body: Write one definition each for monthly recurring revenue, churn, activation and the cost of winning a customer. Use the same definitions in every report so that this month can be compared with last month.
  - title: Pick one channel and run it long enough to learn
    body: Choose the single way of finding customers that has already worked once. Run it for a fixed number of weeks so that you can compare results rather than reacting to one good or bad week.
  - title: Count cost and result for each channel
    body: Each period, record what you spent in money and time, and how many customers you won. Use the written definitions from step one so the figures stay comparable.
  - title: Fix the leaks before adding traffic
    body: Look at onboarding, activation and support before you pour more people in. A leak in the first week costs more than a small gain in reach.
  - title: Write the process down so someone else could run it
    body: Record each step of the channel in plain words, with examples. If it only works when you do it, it is a habit, not a process.
  - title: Review monthly and decide
    body: Once a month, look at the results and choose to put more into a channel, change it or stop. Write the reason down and let the record carry into your investor updates.

## What good looks like

Repeatable growth means the result does not depend on one person having a good week. The same steps, measured the same way, give you figures you can compare from one period to the next.

- Each metric has one written definition with a date, and the definition changes only through a dated note.
- One channel has been run for a fixed period and measured the same way for at least two periods.
- The first week of a new customer's experience has been looked at before more traffic was sent to it.
- A person who did not build the channel can follow the written process and tell you what they did.
- Each monthly review ends in a decision to put more in, change the channel or stop, with the reason beside it.

<Callout title="Illustrative example, not a real company">

A founder with a scheduling tool for tutoring businesses has won customers through direct messages to tutoring owners. She writes one definition each for monthly recurring revenue, churn, activation and the cost of winning a customer, and dates the sheet. She runs the messages channel for a fixed set of weeks and logs the money, the hours and the customers won in each period. In the review she sees that many new customers never finish setup. She pauses the channel, fixes the setup steps and runs it again. In the second period she compares like with like, because the definitions did not change.

</Callout>

## Evidence to check before you move on

Move to <MaybeLink path="/playbooks/prepare-for-investor-conversations/">Prepare for investor conversations</MaybeLink> when you can show each of these.

- The metrics sheet exists, with one definition per measure and a date.
- The channel log covers at least two periods, measured the same way.
- Someone who did not build the channel could follow the written process.
- Monthly reviews record a decision and the reason for it.

These records are what the <MaybeLink path="/fundability/investor-updates/">investor updates guide</MaybeLink> asks you to report from, and what a <MaybeLink path="/fundability/data-room/">seed data room</MaybeLink> holds. Keep them current and they cost almost nothing to share.
