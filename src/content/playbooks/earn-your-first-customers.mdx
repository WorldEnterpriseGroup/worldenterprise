steps:
  - title: Name your first ten
    body: List ten specific people or companies by name, drawn from your interviews and from the problem brief. A named list is something you can work through. A market is not.
  - title: Reach out personally, with a specific reason
    body: Write to each one about the problem you heard from them or from people like them. Say what you would like to try together and what you would ask of them.
  - title: Scope a pilot in writing
    body: Agree what the pilot covers, how long it runs and what result would count as success. Write it down with the customer so both sides can check it at the end.
  - title: Shorten the time to first value
    body: Walk each new customer from sign-up to the first result, and note every point where they hesitate. Time to first value is the time between starting and getting something worth having.
  - title: Ask for the commitment
    body: At the end of the pilot ask for what a real customer would give, whether that is payment, a renewal, a referral or a written reference. A clear no teaches you as much as a yes.
  - title: Log every conversation
    body: After each conversation record what they asked for, what they objected to and why they said yes or no. Review the log weekly for patterns and change the product or the offer when the patterns are clear.

## What good looks like

Good early selling is slow, specific and written down. You can name each person you spoke with, say what you asked of them and say what they answered, including the nos.

- The named list has ten or fewer entries at first, and each one has a status and the date of the last contact.
- Each pilot has a one-page scope that the customer has seen, with an end date and a result that counts as success.
- A new customer can get from sign-up to the first result, and you know where each one hesitated.
- At the end of a pilot you asked for what a real customer gives, such as payment, a renewal, a referral or a written reference.
- The conversation log is current, and you can point to a pattern in it and name it.

<Callout title="Illustrative example, not a real company">

A founder with a tool that reconciles payouts for small online shops lists ten shop owners she interviewed earlier. She writes to each about the exact spreadsheet problem that person described. Four reply and two agree to a pilot. Each pilot has a one-page scope that says what is covered, when it ends and what result counts as success. At the end of the first pilot she asks the shop owner to start paying. The owner says no, because the tool does not yet handle one of the payment providers the shop uses. She writes that reason in the log. When a second owner gives the same reason, she moves that provider to the top of the backlog.

</Callout>

## Evidence to check before you move on

Move to <MaybeLink path="/playbooks/run-your-first-outbound-sequence/">Run a first outbound sequence</MaybeLink> when you can show each of these.

- The named list exists, and every name has a status and a date of last contact.
- At least one pilot ran to its written end, and you asked for a commitment at the close.
- The onboarding checklist shows the steps a new customer follows to the first result.
- The conversation log has enough entries that you can point to a pattern and name it.

If the log shows no pattern, go back to your assumptions in <MaybeLink path="/playbooks/validate-the-problem/">Validate the problem</MaybeLink> before you spend on growth.
