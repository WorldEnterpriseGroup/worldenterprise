This playbook does not give legal advice about marketing messages. The rules depend on the kind of message, who receives it and where they are. Your own counsel tells you which ones apply to you.

## What good looks like

A good first sequence is small, personal and careful. You can say why you wrote to each person, you can show what you sent and when, and nobody who asked you to stop received another message.

- Every person on the list has a stated reason, and the reason would hold up if the person asked.
- The message is short, says who you are and asks for one small thing.
- The sender details, the postal address and the way to opt out were set up and checked before the first send.
- The log holds every send, every reply and every opt-out, with dates.
- Each batch ends with a review and one change you can name.

<Callout title="Illustrative example, not a real company">

A founder of a tool that tracks warranty claims for appliance repair shops builds a list of twenty shop owners from the interviews she did earlier. Beside each name she writes the reason, such as a comment about chasing manufacturers for payment. Her message is four sentences and asks for a short call. She asks her lawyer which rules apply and sets up the sender details and the opt-out before the first message goes. Two owners reply, one asks to stop, and she records all three in the log. In the next batch she changes only the opening line, so she can tell whether it made a difference.

</Callout>

## What the FTC's compliance guide says

The Federal Trade Commission publishes a compliance guide for businesses on the CAN-SPAM Act. Among its points, it says not to use false or misleading header information, and it says your message must include your valid physical postal address. It also says to tell recipients how to opt out of receiving future marketing email from you, and that you must honor a recipient's opt-out request within 10 business days.

The guide is about email. Rules for other channels, and rules in other countries, can differ. Ask your counsel which apply to the messages you plan to send, and treat the guide as a list of questions, not a ruling on your case.

## Evidence to check before you move on

Move to <MaybeLink path="/playbooks/build-repeatable-growth/">Build repeatable growth</MaybeLink> when you can show each of these.

- The list shows a reason for every name and a status for every person.
- Your counsel has told you which sending rules apply to your messages.
- The log holds every send, reply and opt-out, with dates, and no opt-out was followed by another message.
- At least one full batch ran its whole sequence, and the review names the change you made next.
