This playbook does not give legal or tax advice, and it does not tell you which structure to choose. It helps you arrive at your own counsel and accountant with clear questions and clean records. They give the advice.

## What good looks like

Good company setup is dull and well documented. A stranger who reads your folder can tell who owns what, which agreements are signed and which questions are still open.

- Each founder's contribution is written down, including anything that existed before the company did.
- Every question you took to counsel has a written answer, or is marked open with a date.
- Signed agreements sit in one folder with an index, and the copies are the signed ones.
- Your cap table matches your counsel's records, and the date of the last check is on it.
- You know which registrations the company needs, who is responsible for each and whether it is done.

<Callout title="Illustrative example, not a real company">

Two friends plan a billing tool for veterinary clinics. One has written most of the early prototype in evenings while employed elsewhere. The other has the clinic contacts. They have not written down who owns what. Before they hire anyone to write code, they list their contributions, mark the prototype as an open question and note that one of them has an employment agreement to read. They write a page of questions on entity, equity, vesting and ownership of the prototype, and send it to a lawyer they have hired. The meeting is shorter, because the lawyer starts from their list.

</Callout>

## The tax identifier

The IRS says that businesses, organizations and some retirement trusts need an employer identification number (EIN) to manage their taxes, and its list of reasons to get one includes hiring employees. Whether and when your company needs one, and which other registrations apply, is a question for your accountant. Ask early, since the answer affects what you do before your first hire.

## Evidence to check before you move on

Move to <MaybeLink path="/playbooks/build-and-ship-v1/">Build and ship version one</MaybeLink> when you can show each of these.

- The contribution list exists and has been read by every founder.
- Your counsel has answered each question on your list, or the open ones carry a date.
- Signed agreements are in one folder with an index.
- Your cap table matches your counsel's records, with the check dated.
