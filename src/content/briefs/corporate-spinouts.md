Teams inside established companies often have a product idea that sits awkwardly beside the main business. This brief looks at where such a product can live and treats the choice as a legal and organizational decision as much as a product one.

It compares four routes, running from a small team inside the parent to a separately owned company with outside shareholders, and a fourth in which the parent licenses its technology to someone else. For each route it reports what public sources say about the mechanism and then separates that report from analysis.

The same questions run through every route. Who holds title to the code the team writes? Can staff who move hold shares? What can the parent still decide? Which public programs count a parent's ownership against a new venture? What is at stake for the parent's own shareholders if shares are distributed to them? What has to be separated in systems and data?

The brief ends with questions to put to counsel and with a plain statement of the limits of its sources, which are federal rules plus one state's company law. No company is named, no particular arrangement is described and no corporate client is claimed.

This brief is general information for corporate teams. It is not legal, tax or investment advice.
