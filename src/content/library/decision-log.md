## 1. How to use this log

A decision log gives a team and its readers one place to see what was decided and why. It matters most for choices that are hard to reverse: pricing, hiring, ownership, scope and anything you sign. Write the reason at the time, because memory improves the story.

1. Add one row for each decision that someone might later ask about.
2. Write the options you considered, including the one you rejected, and the reason for the choice.
3. Link the evidence: a note, a table, a test result or a document.
4. Set a date to look at the decision again.
5. Never rewrite history. Mark a row superseded and add a new one.

## 4. What belongs in the log

- Pricing and packaging changes.
- What is in and out of a release, and why.
- Hiring and contractor decisions, including who owns what work.
- Choices about customers: who you will and will not sell to.
- Any agreement you sign, and the questions you took to your counsel first.
- Security and data choices, such as which vendor holds customer data.

## 5. Checks

- \[ \] Each row was written when the decision was made.
- \[ \] Each row names who decided.
- \[ \] Each row has a reason, not only an outcome.
- \[ \] Each row links to evidence or says there was none.
- \[ \] Each row has a review date.
- \[ \] Superseded rows are kept.
- \[ \] The log is stored with your other company records.
