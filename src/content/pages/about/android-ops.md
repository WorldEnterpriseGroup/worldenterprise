ANDROID Ops is WE's description of how a team works on a project, which WE calls a droid. It draws on ordinary software delivery practice, including traditional life-cycle planning, agile working, DevOps and machine-learning operations, and arranges it as seven focus areas. The name is an acronym of the areas: Advance, Network, Design, Research, Optimize, Implement, Deliver.

It is a way to organise attention, not a promise of results. A team is not meant to move through the seven areas once, in order; it returns to them as the work demands.

## The seven focus areas

### 1. Advance

Look ahead. The team keeps the roadmap current, generates ideas for features and services, watches industry trends and writes the longer strategy the droid follows.

### 2. Network

Connect people and information. The team sets up the tools for working together at a distance, keeps shared documentation, talks with stakeholders on a regular rhythm, and maps resources and dependencies so they can be seen.

### 3. Design

Shape how the work runs and what it produces. This covers refining processes, designing the user experience, keeping design consistent with the brand, and building sustainability considerations into the work.

### 4. Research

Find out before deciding. The team analyses data, studies customers and their needs, looks at competitors, and explores new technologies and methods.

### 5. Optimize

Improve what already exists. The team defines the indicators that show progress, tunes algorithms, automates repetitive tasks and holds regular reviews so improvement becomes a habit.

### 6. Implement

Put the plan into practice. The team tests quality, has real users try the result, trains the people who will use it, and fits new processes and technology into what is already there.

### 7. Deliver

Get value to the customer and keep it there. The team listens to customer feedback, manages releases, checks that what ships meets what was promised, and supports it after release.

## The Focus Shift

A Focus Shift is a four-hour block given to one droid. In it, the team chooses one or more of the seven focus areas and works on them to shift the droid's trajectory. The plan for a Focus Shift is judged on four points: fit with the droid's objectives, clarity, feasibility within four hours, and likely impact.
