WE steers a team with a small set of frameworks that it wrote for its own use. This page explains each in your words, not WE's jargon. Two have pages of their own: ANDROID Ops, which describes how a team works, and ORBITAL, which describes how a project is planned.

These are working methods. They are not industry standards, they have not been independently evaluated, and WE does not claim that using them produces any particular result. Their value is shared language: when someone says what a team is doing, everyone hears the same thing.

## The vocabulary: droids and worlds

WE calls a project a **droid** and a company a **world**. A droid is launched with a single purpose. As it proves itself, it can grow into a world of its own or join one that already exists. The program follows the same path for a SaaS idea: a project that begins as a question and, with evidence, becomes a company.

The same vocabulary scales up. WE has words for groups of companies and whole industries, but a founder in this program only needs the first two.

## UBF, the Universal Business Framework

UBF describes business through relationships rather than org charts. It names the kinds of bodies you find in an industry: the industry itself, very large companies, holding companies, flagship companies, small companies, side projects and projects that are unstable and need better planning. Seen this way, a new project has neighbors, dependencies and a place on a map.

UBF also sets out a five-stage lifecycle for an initiative: ideation and conceptualization, planning and resource allocation, execution and monitoring, scaling and growth, and integration and ecosystem management. WE uses it to keep the larger picture in view while a team works on a small piece of it.

## STAR, for daily task reporting

STAR is a reporting habit. A task is first written to be specific, measurable, achievable, relevant and timed. Then it is described through four lenses: **Space**, where the task sits and what it depends on; **Track**, where it has been and where it goes next; **Assist**, what is being done today to move it; and **Reach**, what the finished task is expected to change. A person can read a STAR report in a minute and know where the work stands.

## SHIFT, the Focus Shift

A Focus Shift is a dedicated four-hour block spent on one droid. During the block the team picks one or more of the seven ANDROID Ops activities and works only on them, to move that droid back toward its goal. A plan for a Focus Shift is read against four questions: does it fit the droid's objectives, is it clear, is it feasible in four hours, and what difference could it make.
