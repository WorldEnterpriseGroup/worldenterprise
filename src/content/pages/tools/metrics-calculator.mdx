## Your numbers stay on this page

The calculator works in your browser. It sends nothing, it saves nothing, and it does not put your numbers in the address bar, so closing the tab removes them. The page makes no request after it loads, and you can confirm that in your browser's network panel.

## What the results are not

The results are arithmetic on the numbers you typed. They are not advice, a valuation, a forecast, or a statement about whether anyone will invest, and WE makes no promise of funding. The calculator cannot tell whether an entry is right, and it shows no benchmark or target, because the research it draws on publishes none. A figure that looks good here is only as sound as the books behind it.

## Report the same way every time

Two reports of "churn" cannot be compared unless they use the same definition. The research brief on early-stage SaaS metrics asks you to answer six questions in writing for every metric you report:

1. What is the period?
2. Who is in the population?
3. Are one-time fees excluded?
4. Which formula variant is used?
5. Has the definition changed since the last report?
6. Can a reader recompute the number from the inputs given?

This calculator answers the first and the last for you: it works on one month, and it shows the working so a reader can recompute each result.

## Where the formulas come from

Net revenue retention, customer acquisition cost and customer lifetime value use the formulas published in the [research brief on SaaS metrics definitions](/research/early-stage-saas-metrics-definitions/). The brief does not define net new MRR, gross revenue churn, the quick ratio, CAC payback, the ratio of lifetime value to CAC or runway. For those six, the calculator uses the form in which each is commonly calculated. That is a convention, not a WE method, and the table above links a public page that states each one. Every result carries a label that says which kind it is, and the table lists the formula and assumptions behind every one.
