The playbooks follow the order in which a SaaS company is usually built: validate the problem, design the product, set up the company, build and ship version one, hire the first team member, price the product, earn the first customers, run a first outbound sequence, build growth that repeats, and prepare for investor conversations. They sit across three stages: Dream, Build and Grow.

Each playbook is a short sequence of steps. Every step names the thing you should have in hand when it is done, so you can tell the difference between finishing a step and merely talking about it. Each playbook also says what good looks like, offers a decision aid, lists the mistakes that cost founders the most time, gives an evidence check to run before you move on and ends with the sources it relied on.

You do not need to read them in order, but the later ones assume the earlier work exists. If you cannot yet write down who has the problem and what they do about it today, start with the first playbook, not the last.

The playbooks are general working guides. They are not a promise about how any company will perform, and they do not replace advice from your own lawyer, accountant or other qualified adviser. The playbooks on company setup, hiring and investors say only what to ask your own counsel. WE makes no promise of funding.

Each worked example is invented and labeled as an illustrative example, not a real company.
