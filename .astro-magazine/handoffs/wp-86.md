## How it works

- The chain is data. `liveSequence(isLive)` filters `PLAYBOOK_SEQUENCE` by the route state of the build, `nextLive` points each page at the next live playbook and the last live one is the terminal. A playbook that is not live drops out of the hub, the trail and the "next" link, so no page links to one that does not exist.
- Templates, tools and briefs go through `artifactRefs` and then `liveItems`, so a route that is not live is never linked (fail-closed). A template slug that no page owns yet is not linked at all.
- The hub lead is `PLAYBOOKS_HUB_LEAD` in `meta.ts`, so the sentence ledger can cover it as a `.ts` document (a `.astro` document breaks the wp-60 frontmatter parse).
- The five original playbooks keep their photo slots (`pb-1` to `pb-5`). The five new ones have no photo and their hero uses the trail art. No image was added.
- Illustrative examples are invented, carry no names or figures that could read as a WE company, and map to W8-24 as considerations.
- The legal, hiring and funding playbooks say only what to ask counsel. The investor playbook carries "WE makes no promise of funding." (C-05) and never names the add-on.

## Decisions

- Titles: the wp-15 test measures a title plus the site suffix against 60 characters and the playbook schema has no `seoTitle`, so three new titles were shortened to "Set up the company", "Run a first outbound sequence" and "Prepare for investor talks". The slugs, the order and the headings of the pages are unchanged. The hub body copy still says "investor conversations".
- External facts (seven pages from irs.gov, uscis.gov, ftc.gov, sec.gov, w3.org and nist.gov) were fetched with WebFetch on 2026-10-10 and recorded with a verbatim quote of at most 25 words each. A sentence that states what an outside page says maps to W8-9 and has an entry in `facts`. The IRS contractor page was re-fetched and the copy changed to its own category wording.
- No amounts, prices, seat counts, response-time promises or application counts anywhere in the ten files.
