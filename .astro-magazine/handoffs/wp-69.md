## Decisions made

- Attribution. Every statement about a vendor or a model is attributed to the vendor's page fetched on October 10, 2026. No price, benchmark, ranking, latency or limit figure appears in any article. The metering article says outright that it quotes no prices or limits. Claims sourced from vendor documentation hosts are labeled `paraphrase` (never `statistic` or `regulation`), as the sources ledger rule requires.
- The NIST AI RMF and the EU AI Act are described from their primary pages (nvlpubs.nist.gov, airc.nist.gov, nist.gov, eur-lex.europa.eu, digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu), not from commentary.
- EU AI Act currency. The original 2024 text in the Official Journal is quoted for definitions and article wording; the AI Omnibus, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, entered into force on July 27, 2026 and moved the high-risk dates and rewrote Article 4. The B article says this in its own section and tells the reader to confirm any article against the consolidated text. The human review article quotes the original text too and says so in "What this article cannot tell you".
- State law is limited to Colorado (Attorney General page, SB 26-189 and HB 26-1263) and Texas (enrolled HB 149 and its bill history) because their primary pages could be fetched. The B article says that other states and any federal law are not reviewed.
- Texas wording. The enrolled text says the Act takes effect January 1, 2026, and the Texas Legislature Online bill history, fetched October 10, 2026, lists "Signed by the Governor" on 06/22/2025 and a last action of "Effective on 1/1/26". The article reports those two sources and does not say the statute "is in force" in its own voice. Likewise "transparency is the tier most likely to touch a SaaS product" is marked as the article's own reading.
- Nothing says WE builds, hosts or evaluates a model for a client, and nothing says WE follows, conforms to or is certified under any framework. Every article states what it cannot tell you, and the B article is general information, not legal advice.
- `relatedServices`. Service B (Product and IT Development) on the eight build articles, the nearest line to building a product on a model; service G (Professional-Services Coordination) on the regulation article. The rail link is a pointer to the service page, not a statement about models. Change the letters if the integrator prefers another line.
- Internal links are Markdown links (`[text](/path/)`), because the WP-60 rubric counts `](/path)` and `href="/path"` and does not count the `MaybeLink` component. Each article has at least four, all to paths in the routes ledger (the nine articles, `/library/security-baseline-checklist/`, `/playbooks/build-and-ship-v1/`, `/research/saas-security-baseline-year-one/`, `/glossary/`).
- Frontmatter. `seoTitle` is at most 31 characters (the schema cap of 60 minus the 29-character title suffix), and every `title`, `seoTitle` and `description` that contains a colon is quoted so the YAML parses.

## Open questions and follow-ups

1. Desk page in the sources ledger (affects every desk package). Contract section 9 says a page's sentences go in `documents[].sentences[]`. `checkSourcesFile` runs its source rules over every `documents[]` entry, and a `pages/` file has no `sources` and cannot carry `noStatistics` (the `pages` schema is strict), so an entry for `topic-ai.mdx` fails with "has 0 distinct sources and is not noStatistics". `wp-69.json` therefore keeps the desk page's sentences in a top-level `sentences` list (`document`, `text`, `page`; the orchestrator asked for `documents[].sentences[]` with the key `text`, and the entries move there as soon as the checker allows it) and lists only the nine articles under `documents`. If a sentence-ledger test exists or is added, it needs to read that list, or `checkSourcesFile` needs to skip entries that carry `sentences` and no `claims`. The `wp-81` and `wp-83` ledgers carry their desk pages under `documents` and will hit the same failure.
2. Host allowlist (OP-HOSTS). The `wp-60` snapshot that was read holds an allowlist without six hosts, so `checkSourcesFile` reports 15 allowlist-only problems for this package. 60a needs to add: `platform.claude.com`, `privacy.claude.com`, `developers.openai.com` and `ai.google.dev` as vendor documentation hosts; `arxiv.org` and `aclanthology.org` as scholarly hosts. They are on the approved list for this wave. Every other host is already classed: `genai.owasp.org`, the `.gov` and `.europa.eu` hosts, `learn.microsoft.com` and `docs.aws.amazon.com` (vendor class, `paraphrase` claims only).
