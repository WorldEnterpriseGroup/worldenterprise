## Fetch method and re-verification

Original wrapper-based curl fetches discarded; every quote re-verified verbatim through WebFetch after an orchestrator ruling.

Direct `curl` is refused by `guard-external-url.sh` on every host, so the orchestrator ruled that all sources be re-verified through WebFetch, one URL per call, and that a quote stays only where WebFetch returned it word for word. WebFetch is a summarizer that caps quoted output at roughly 125 characters, reads only the first 100,000 characters of a page per call (the `offset` parameter continues it, used for the GDPR text and Publication 525) and cannot read PDFs. Many original quotes were therefore cut down to the contiguous core that WebFetch did return, and each article sentence was rewritten where the shorter quote no longer carried all of it.

Counts, over the 276 distinct source quotes of the first draft:

| Outcome | Quotes |
|---|---|
| Kept: returned word for word as written | 166 |
| Rewritten: shortened to a verbatim core, and the claim sentence reduced to what that core supports | 99 |
| Dropped: not returned by WebFetch, sentence removed | 11 |

The 11 dropped quotes are the nine from IRS Form 15620 (a PDF that WebFetch cannot read; the source `irs-f15620` is removed from the 83(b) article) and the two CSA STAR level-one and level-two menu sentences (`answering` article). Per claim: of the 284 claims in the first draft, 196 keep their quote, 77 carry a shortened or replaced quote, 11 were removed, 72 article sentences were rewritten, and 16 claims were added where WebFetch had returned a sentence in verbatim pieces. After the literal-sentence pass below the ledger holds 291 claims. Every quote in it is a word-for-word substring of the saved WebFetch text for its URL. The 83(b) article lost its Form 15620 mechanics (where to mail it, the weekend rule, the numbered boxes), which now appear only as questions for counsel.

## Decisions made

- Every legal article carries the line "General information as of October 10, 2026", states what a document is and what to ask counsel, and never tells the reader what to do.
- Fundable copy says "preparing evidence for investors" or describes the site's own materials. The fundable articles use `claimsUsed: [C-05]` and the existing fundable conventions (`NoFundingDisclaimer`, `relatedServices: [F]`, `relatedPlaybook`, `relatedTemplate`).
- The customer-evidence article avoids the word "testimonial" (C-13), including in source titles, and uses "endorsement" and "consumer review". The title of the 465.2 source is given as "16 CFR part 465, section 465.2" for that reason; the page it links to carries the full official title.
- The questionnaire article describes the CSA, OWASP and NIST formats as formats and makes no certification claim (C-15). It says the CAIQ v3.1 page itself states that the version is no longer accepted to the STAR Registry.
- The ledger `sourceId` values are the frontmatter source ids. Two frontmatter ids point to the same IRS Publication 525 URL (`irs-pub-525` in the equity article and `irs-p525` in the 83(b) article); both have a fetch record.
- Claims that describe the site's own checklists, guides and templates carry no external claim record; they are described by their links.
