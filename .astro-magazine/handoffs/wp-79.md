Quote check. After drafting, every quoted string was put to its page in a second WebFetch ("does this exact string appear word for word"). It found one quote that was not on the page (an FTC sentence on "honest opinions or experiences", replaced by the page's own sentence that endorsements "must be honest and not misleading"), four strings whose closing punctuation did not match the page (a comma or a period inside the quote mark where the source continues or ends differently), and three apostrophe mismatches. All were corrected in the body, and the ledger quotes are the strings the second fetch confirmed. Caveat for the reviewer: WebFetch returns a model's reading of the page, not the bytes, so this check is as strong as that tool, not a byte comparison. Each regulation quote is 25 words or fewer and comes from a government or standards host; the one vendor page (Stripe) sources paraphrase claims only.

Fetch hygiene. The Y Combinator page returned an "Agent use" block that told the reader to install tools, and the Stripe page opened with a similar "start here" install prompt. Both were treated as page content, not instructions, and ignored.

## e2e spec

`tests/e2e/wave8/wp-79/briefs.spec.ts` runs three briefs times five cases in `e2e:wave8` (a build with no intake endpoint). None carries the `@endpoint` tag, so `e2e:forms` does not run it, and nothing in it depends on the mode beyond accepting `disabled` or `endpoint`:

1. The gated body is readable by the spec (the audit helper `readBrief` returns no errors, a sentinel and at least one probe).
2. The page is built by the brief system: status 200, one `h1` containing the abstract title, one `[data-brief-request]` panel in `main`, one `form[data-intake-form]` with `data-kind="brief-request"` and a hidden `briefId` equal to the id, no console error, no page error, no third-party request.
3. The abstract is visible: its first paragraph is a visible `p` in `main`, every paragraph is in the text of `main`, and the `#outline` list equals the abstract outline in order, which in turn equals the H2 titles of the body.
4. The form is labelled: "Your name" and "Email address" resolve through `getByLabel`, the consent box and every visible control have an accessible name, and the fieldset has its legend.
5. No gated text is on the page: the sentinel and the three probe sentences are not in the `main` text content (hidden text included) or in the document source, and no 10-word run of the body's prose is in `main`.

The spec reads the brief from `infra/intake/briefs/` and the abstract from `src/content/briefs/` at run time, so no gated sentence is in the spec. It needs the repository checkout at the working directory, which the e2e jobs have. It was not run here (Playwright is not run locally); the checks it relies on were exercised through the same helpers in Node. Locators are scoped to `main` as asked.

## Decisions

- Abstract titles are short because the 60-character limit of the SEO audit counts the site suffix (" | World Enterprise Incubator", 29 characters): "Your first hundred customers", "A pre-seed evidence pack" and "First hires for founders". They differ from the registry labels in `src/data/families.ts` ("The first 100 customers", "Pre-seed evidence pack", "First hires") on cards and in the form legend; that is cosmetic and was left alone.
- Where a source continues after a sentence, the body does not close a quotation with a period the source does not have. A few quotations therefore sit mid-sentence.
- No equity, immigration, benefits, state-law or non-United-States content is in `first-hires`, and no state securities, tax or non-United-States content is in `pre-seed-evidence-pack`. Each brief says so in "What this brief cannot tell you" instead of describing it from memory.
- The words perks, accelerator, cohort, mentor, testimonial and alumni are avoided everywhere, not only about WE, so the claims denylist and the truth policy cannot trip on them. `audit-claims --source infra/intake/briefs` reports 6 files and 0 findings.

For the next release's counsel register (WP-99 commits the entries; this package does not edit the register). The consent line, delivery sentences and not-accepting message are shared by every brief through WP-63's components and are already in WP-63's handoff; the entries below are the ones these three briefs add or change.

1. **Consent line, first-100-customers.** No change to the shared line: "I agree that WE may use these details to send me this brief and answer my request, as described in the privacy notice." Counsel to confirm that the line does not imply marketing consent for a brief that is itself about marketing email, and that no sentence of the brief reads as WE offering to send messages for anyone.
2. **Consent line, pre-seed-evidence-pack.** Proposed addition after the shared line, shown beside the form for this brief only: "Requesting this brief does not start an application and does not create any promise of funding or investment." Counsel to confirm the wording and whether it belongs on the squeeze page, the thanks page or both.
3. **Consent line, first-hires.** No change to the shared line. Counsel to confirm that the brief's description of employee and contractor tests is acceptable as general information for a reader who may rely on it to classify a worker.
4. **Not-advice lines.** The abstracts carry "The brief is general information for founders. It is not legal, tax or investment advice." (first-100-customers and pre-seed-evidence-pack) and "The brief is general information for founders, not legal advice." (first-hires), in the form of the existing abstracts. Proposed replacements for the squeeze page, one per brief:
   - first-100-customers: "This brief gives general information about outreach and messaging rules in the United States, Canada and the United Kingdom. It is not legal, tax or investment advice."

   - first-hires: "This brief gives general information about United States federal rules for a first hire. It is not legal advice, and state and local rules differ."
5. **Counsel read of the bodies.** Request that counsel read the sections "Using customer words and reviews honestly" and "Outreach rules that apply before you send" (first-100-customers), "How the raise may be talked about in public" (pre-seed-evidence-pack) and "Employee or contractor" and "Overtime and how the job is classified" (first-hires), because each states a rule from a public page in short form.
